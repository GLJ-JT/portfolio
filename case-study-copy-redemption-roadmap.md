# Case Study Copy Redemption Roadmap

## North Star

Rewrite every case with the thinking quality behind the Depology benchmark:

> "The diagnosis moved from “which screen is underperforming?” to “which repeated decisions are users struggling to make?” I looked at how users arrived, where they paused, what they tapped, and which parts of the journey created uncertainty."

This does not mean copying the sentence structure. The benchmark matters because it shows a way of thinking: the surface problem is questioned, the real repeated decision is named, the designer explains what they inspected, and the copy makes uncertainty visible.

The new portfolio voice should sound like a designer who watched the work closely, understood the true problem, made specific choices, and can explain what the outcome did and did not prove. It should not sound like a narrator trying to make every project feel huge.

## Rewrite Rule

Every important paragraph should carry a useful piece of reasoning. Across a case study, the reader should understand:

1. What the true problem was, not just the obvious surface request.
2. Why Joe chose this direction instead of another plausible direction.
3. How Joe thought about the user, client, operator, business, or system constraint.
4. What changed in the interface, content, system, brand, or flow.
5. What the outcome was, with proof level clearly separated from assumption.
6. What Joe would inspect next if the work continued.

If a paragraph answers none of these, it is probably filler.

## Paragraph Model

Do not force every case into the same sentence pattern. Use the following as a thinking model, not a copy template.

### 1. True Problem

Explain the hidden problem behind the brief.

Weak: "The site needed to look more premium."

Better: "The old site could take a payment, but it did not answer the questions a foreign traveller needed answered before trusting the trip."

### 2. Reasoning

Explain how Joe looked at the problem.

Useful evidence can include heatmaps, session length, click/tap behaviour, stakeholder review notes, existing page inventory, test tasks, support/sales questions, prototype stress cases, or before/after production assets. The important thing is not naming evidence for decoration; it is showing how the evidence changed the design judgement.

### 3. Design Choice

Explain what Joe changed and why.

"I removed the custom booking portal from the first release and replaced it with an eligibility form plus deposit link."

### 4. Tradeoff

Every strong case admits at least one tradeoff:

"This was less automated than a full booking engine, but it let the startup launch without freezing the product for months."

### 5. Outcome And Next Inspection

Use proof only where it exists. If the result is qualitative, say so. Then state what should be inspected next.

"In stakeholder review, this gave the founder a launchable sales surface." is more credible than "The results validated every decision."

"The next thing to inspect would be where parents leave the eligibility form and which programme recommendations lead to deposit clicks." is more useful than pretending the case already proves conversion.

## Priority 2: Worldschooling

### Current Problem

The case hides a strong UX decision under startup cliches. The real work was not "transformative educational experiences"; it was reducing scope without breaking the buying journey.

### Redemption Goal

Make the case about parent/student decision-making and launch constraint.

### Evidence Needed

- Original planned architecture.
- Final release architecture.
- Programme tier model.
- Parent/student questions before committing.
- Any testing detail behind NPS or engagement claims. If no hard values exist, remove those claims.

### Rewrite Direction

Thinking anchor:

The case should explain why the full education-travel platform was too much for the launch window, then show how Joe narrowed the first release around the parent/student decisions that had to happen before a deposit: comparison, eligibility, trust, and commitment.

"I replaced the first-release booking portal with a simpler path: programme discovery, tier comparison, detail page, eligibility form, and universal deposit link. It was less automated, but it kept the important commitment steps visible and removed months of portal development."

Remove or rewrite:

- "timing is everything"
- "compelling vision"
- "transformative experiences"
- "results validated every decision"
- "founder was thrilled"
- "proof that the company could deliver on its promise"

## Priority 4: Worldover.io

### Current Problem

The case has a strong UX principle: keep tabs because each tab protects a separate mental mode. The copy should make that operational, not theoretical.

### Redemption Goal

Make the case about compliance operators clearing threshold risk under deadline pressure.

### Evidence Needed

- One concrete entity example from the dummy data.
- What changes when switching entity.
- Which report blocker appears.
- Which evidence item is missing or stale.
- What action closes or advances the case.

### Rewrite Direction

Thinking anchor:

The case should explain why adding more compliance data was not enough. It should show how an operator moves between risk status, production contribution, alert rules, report blockers, and evidence records, and why those jobs need separate work modes.

Design decision paragraph:

"I kept the multi-tab model because the work changes by tab. Risk Command is where the operator decides the next action. Production explains contribution. Alerts manage escalation rules. Reporting shows what blocks a draft. Evidence proves whether the record can survive review."

Remove or rewrite:

- "high-consequence compliance space"
- "different kinds of thinking"
- "operational lens"
- "scannable decision workflow"
- "production-facing interaction design"

## Priority 5: Explainable Property Search

### Current Problem

The local docs are promising and honest, but the portfolio-facing summary sounds like a technical feature label.

### Redemption Goal

Turn the case into a product judgement story about messy renter intent and visible system understanding.

### Evidence Needed

- Three messy user queries.
- Parsed chips produced from each query.
- One false-positive risk and how the system avoids it.
- One ranking explanation.
- Stress test results, with caveat that these are prototype tests.

### Rewrite Direction

Thinking anchor:

The case should explain why natural-language search only helps if the renter can see what the system understood. Use messy queries where budget, commute, station proximity, exclusions, and soft preferences are mixed together in one sentence.

Design decision paragraph:

"The prototype keeps normal filters, but adds a visible interpretation layer. The query becomes editable chips, ranked listings explain their match, and hard exclusions stay visible so the user can catch mistakes before trusting the results."

Keep:

- "Trust drops quickly when the system returns obvious false positives."
- "The same intent can appear as a positive request, a negative constraint, or a soft preference."

## Portfolio-Wide Rewrite Workflow

### Pass 1: Delete Inflated Claims

Remove every sentence that claims transformation, validation, resonance, or strategic importance without proof.

### Pass 2: Add Behaviour

For each case, add at least one sentence naming what someone did:

- tapped
- paused
- compared
- abandoned
- asked
- selected
- switched entity
- checked evidence
- submitted form
- paid deposit
- saved search

### Pass 3: Add Tradeoffs

Every project should contain one tradeoff. This makes the designer sound more senior and more honest.

Examples:

- "I kept the payment link manual because a custom booking engine would have delayed launch."
- "I kept normal filters because natural language search should augment control, not replace it."
- "I kept separate tabs because collapsing the flow made operators compare unrelated evidence at once."

### Pass 4: Replace Jargon With Interface Facts

Use this conversion table:

| Slop phrase | Replace with |
| --- | --- |
| End-to-end framework delivery | The surfaces delivered: site, identity, content system, forms, templates |
| Seamless flow | The exact steps users move through |
| Strategic UX intervention | The specific component or information-order change |
| Immersive storytelling | The sections, media, and proof points used |
| Scalable system | The reusable rules, templates, tokens, or data model |
| Strong engagement | The metric, test observation, or remove the claim |
| Stakeholders praised | What they approved, changed, or shipped |

### Pass 5: Human Rhythm Edit

Read each paragraph aloud. If it sounds like a LinkedIn announcement, cut it down. Use short sentences after complex ones. Let the work carry the confidence.

## Final Quality Bar

Before publishing, every case should contain:

- One diagnosis shift.
- One concrete user/client/operator behaviour.
- One design decision.
- One tradeoff.
- One proof point or honest limitation.
- Zero unsupported "validated every decision" claims.

The voice should feel like this:

"I understood what was really making the decision unclear. I can explain why I chose this direction. I can show what changed. I know what the outcome proves, what it does not prove, and what I would inspect next."
